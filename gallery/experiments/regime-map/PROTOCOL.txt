REGIME CAMPAIGN — FINITE-TIME TRANSVERSE GROWTH AND MEAN-FLOW ACCURACY

Question
When can the y-averaged wave–current flow follow its autonomous x-z dynamics?
Two distinct measurements are required: growth of transverse dependence, and
its feedback on the mean. Periodicity makes mean(v_y)=0 but does not remove
averaged products or establish closure. In-plane eddy transport is retained.

Physical design
The reference Stokes drift is S exp(2 k z), with k=6.1 m^-1, wave steepness
0.17 and S=0.17^2 sqrt(g/k). UE0=-alpha us_ref, UL0=(waves-alpha)us_ref,
q0=UE0'=-alpha us_ref'. Alpha is 0.5 or 1, plus the previous 0.75 anchor.
The x-z eddy RMS U is 0.0012, 0.004 or 0.012 m/s. Injection wavelength is
0.25 m. The box is 2 x 1 x 0.5 m, periodic x/y and impermeable z.
All initial fields are discretely solenoidal. Matching seeds preserve the
initial y-invariant realization within each physical cell. A transverse
perturbation adds eta times the base eddy energy: eta=0, 1e-6, 1e-8 or 1.
The latter is a finite-disturbance experiment with deliberately added energy,
not an equal-total-energy causal comparison.

Explicit viscosity nu=U*0.25/Re_lambda, Re_lambda=100, accompanies Centered2
advection. A documented compensating acceleration maintains the prescribed
laminar UL0 against viscous diffusion; it does not maintain turbulent eddies.
Its native contribution and the viscous contribution are included in budgets.
This controlled background distinguishes the regime experiment from the
unmaintained finite wave packet. Achieved scales and Re must be diagnosed;
the input reference value is not the Reynolds number of every later state.

Forty-nine integrations
Seven primary cells, each with four perturbation levels (28 runs).
A second seed at low-U/alpha=1 and high-U/alpha=0.5, with 2D/tiny/finite (6).
Anchor tests at Re=300, doubled Ly with doubled Ny, doubled resolution,
half maximum timestep and no waves, each with 2D/tiny/finite (15).
The no-wave runs retain the same initial Eulerian background and maintain it;
they test removal of the vortex force in the presence of that shear.
The width test changes accessible transverse modes and is a physical-span
comparison, not a claim that identical initial transverse fields were used.
All integrations last 600 s. Eddy turnover times and decay vary with U; both
physical time and normalized time are reported. At most two GPUs run at once.

Measurements
1. E_perp/E_eddy and E_perp(t)/E_perp(0), with actual amplitude-normalized
   tiny-disturbance agreement. Growth estimates use only resolved, still-small
   intervals. A saturated disturbance is not evidence for a linear exponent.
   This is a finite-amplitude approximation to transverse linear response,
   checked by two amplitudes; it is not a tangent-linear integration.
   A displayed fitted rate requires a contiguous small-disturbance interval
   (both energy fractions below 1%, normalized amplitude difference <=5%,
   at least five samples, log-amplitude fit RMS <=0.2). These are declared
   response-quality checks, not physical regime boundaries.
2. Signed y-mean U(x,z), W(x,z), Q(x,z) and jet-profile anomalies versus the
   matched 2D trajectory. Retain x-z eddies; record correlations and errors
   normalized by initial and evolving scales. Chaotic trajectory separation
   alone does not prove failure of a mean statistical description.
3. Native full-minus-y-mean momentum advection and its y-curl diagnose direct
   transverse feedback. Viscosity, imposed forcing and the wave term remain
   explicit. Signed projections and cumulative effects complement RMS norms.
4. The continuum scalar equation uses A=mean_y(u q_x+w q_z), and Rbar from
   the omitted 3D vorticity terms. Qt+A=Rbar+viscous/forcing terms is checked
   without replacing A by U Qx+W Qz. Its residual includes derivative,
   interpolation, time-sampling and discrete/continuum differences.
   This Rbar is not the same object as native full-minus-ymean advection curl.
5. Signed transfer across horizontal-x cutoffs, where available, is labelled
   as horizontal-scale transfer. The bounded-depth calculation is not a
   radially isotropic inverse-cascade measurement. Background/mean exchanges
   are retained when using anomaly energy.
6. The existing seven-seed Ellingsen packet audit is assessed separately using
   signed spatial filtering before norms, preserving A and its covariance.
   Derivative-stencil and temporal-sampling sensitivity accompany every
   conclusion. Agreement with a laboratory mean profile does not by itself
   measure the omitted vorticity terms or prove an autonomous 2D regime.

Numerical and operational checks
CPU/GPU agreement, restart equivalence, discrete incompressibility and
impermeable boundaries; exact-2D transverse terms; maintained-laminar null;
viscous/forcing operator consistency; temporal budget integration; short
primary/fine GPU runs; synthetic analysis and publication/receipt tests.
Production source and environment are frozen after validation. Completed-run
files are hashed and backed up to persistent home storage before a completion
receipt permits publication. Native fields, keys and credentials are private.
The public tab receives derived plots, tables, protocol and source provenance.
Failed jobs are reconciled from Slurm; they are not silently shown as running.

Interpretation
Report empirical finite-time trends and numerical sensitivity rather than
assigning universal stable/unstable cutoffs from one ratio. A flow can retain
3D energy yet have weak mean feedback; small energy can still matter through
gradients or resonant interactions. No assumption UL0=0 or q0=0 is made.
The local Stokes shear varies strongly with depth, so a surface proxy alone
cannot place the whole flow in a rotating-fluid regime.

Literature motivating distinct tests
Chen, Chen, Eyink & Holm (2005), JFM 542, 139–164:
https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022112005006324
Compare the averaged 3D solution with matched 2D dynamics, rather than only
measuring the fraction of energy in the invariant plane.
Gallet (2015), JFM 783, 412–447:
https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2015.569
Distinguish linear stability from attraction under finite disturbances;
sufficient bounds for rigid rotation are not our empirical regime boundary.
Seshasayanan & Gallet (2020), JFM 901, R5:
https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2020.541
Transverse growth on an evolving turbulent base depends on Reynolds number
and axial extent. Our freely evolving finite-time assay is shorter and uses
two small amplitudes, so it cannot claim their asymptotic Lyapunov threshold.
