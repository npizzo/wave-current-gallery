The scalar law and the 2D3C null test

Here q = ∂zuL − ∂xw − u′s is the Eulerian transverse component of vorticity. Its equation is DLq = T + Dq, where T = q∂yv + (∂yw)(∂xv) − (∂yu)(∂zv). Dq retains viscosity and the prescribed background-maintaining force. The derivative DL uses the Lagrangian velocity.

Thus Zq = ⟨q²⟩/2 measures one component of enstrophy, not the full vector enstrophy. T vanishes when ∂y=0, including in a 2D3C flow with nonzero v(x,z). Other vorticity components can still be stretched in that flow. The 2D3C cases test precisely this distinction.

Transverse periodicity makes ⟨∂yv⟩ vanish; it does not force ⟨q²∂yv⟩ or the tilting products to vanish. Likewise, −v∂yq is transport, not an independent enstrophy-production mechanism.

Background conversion and mean–variance exchange

The Eulerian background is UE0=−αus,ref+Uoffset, with q0=−αu′s,ref. It is not set to zero. In no-wave controls the actual Stokes drift vanishes while this prescribed Eulerian background remains. The native discrete-center q0 and analytic profile are both exported.

For a=q−q0, DLa=T−wq′0+Dq. Its variance can therefore grow through −⟨awq′0⟩ even when T=0. That background conversion is plotted separately from ⟨aT⟩.

For the transverse mean Q and fluctuation q′=q−Q, Zq=Zmean+Zvariance. With F=(meany(u′q′), meany(w′q′)), the exchange ⟨F·∇Q⟩ enters those two budgets with opposite signs. The production ⟨QT⟩+⟨q′T⟩ changes their sum. Zvariance/Za removes the large imposed background from the denominator, but is not used as a universal regime threshold.

Two independent residuals, including layer transport

The temporal check compares each observed moment change with the trapezoidal time integral of its freshly evaluated native rate. The native q tendency is multiplied by the full q field before averaging; it is not reconstructed from mean vorticity alone.

The spatial check compares that native rate with the continuum production, viscosity, maintenance forcing and transport terms. Discrete derivatives, interpolations and product-rule differences can leave a residual. Both signed residuals are shown explicitly; neither is adjusted to force closure or called pure physical dissipation.

Full-depth and upper Stokes-layer budgets are separate. The layer consists of complete grid cells with centers between −1/(2k) and the surface. Flux through its actual lower face is included. A layer budget without that transport would confuse motion across its boundary with production.

Experimental design and limits

The central background α=0.75 is tested at initial reference Reynolds numbers 100, 200 and 300 with η=0, 10⁻⁸, 10⁻⁶, 0.01 and 1. Additional background, no-wave, 2D3C, finer-grid, half-time-step and second-seed checks test the interpretation. Each run lasts 1200 s. η is the initial transverse-dependent perturbation energy relative to the prescribed 2D eddies; finite disturbances add energy.

All reported currents are Eulerian: UE=UL−us. Profile-change comparisons subtract each run’s own initial horizontal mean. Shared initial fields, settings and output checksums are verified before publication.

This finite-time suite can test production mechanisms and the usefulness of a mean description. It does not alone prove an asymptotic stability boundary, a universal rotation analogue, or a tracer-mixing barrier. The previous regime map and Ellingsen packet comparison remain distinct datasets; their results are not relabelled as outcomes of these new tests.

When is the two-dimensional mean description useful?

These simulations support a useful weak-3D approximation in part of the tested parameter range. In the central background, tiny initial disturbances leave the mean-current evolution close to its matched two-dimensional evolution. Larger disturbances change that evolution substantially. The background vorticity q0 and the accumulated 3D feedback matter: neither the initial disturbance size nor the final 3D energy fraction is sufficient on its own.

This is a result for a finite observation time and the specified decaying flows. Some small disturbances grow strongly while the mean remains close to 2D. The calculations therefore do not establish permanent suppression of 3D motion or a universal Taylor–Proudman threshold.

What “3D-ness” means here

Velocity geometry: χE = E3D/Eeddy. E3D is the energy of velocity remaining after subtracting its transverse y mean; Eeddy subtracts the horizontal x–y mean. A y-independent velocity has χE = 0 even if v(x,z) is nonzero.

Vorticity geometry: χq = ⟨(q−Q)²⟩/⟨(q−q0)²⟩, where Q = meany q. This measures one vorticity component and is included in the downloadable metrics. Subtracting q0 prevents the prescribed shear from dominating its denominator.

Absolute amplification: GE(t) = E3D(t)/E3D(0). A rising fraction χE can reflect decay of the other eddies; GE checks whether the disturbance energy itself grows.

Mean-flow consequence: εU(t) = ‖ΔUE(t) − ΔUE,2D(t)‖z / ‖ΔUE,2D(t)‖z. ΔU is the horizontal-mean Eulerian current change from its own initial profile; ‖·‖z is the depth-weighted RMS. Thus a 100% error is relative to the change predicted by the matched 2D run, not to the entire imposed current. Absolute errors in mm s⁻¹ are also downloadable.

The weak-disturbance distinction survives the available checks. At Reinj = 300 and η = 10⁻⁶, the error is 0.430% on the finer grid and 3.63% for seed 2. For η = 1 it is 99.4% and 84.2%, respectively. Yet the tiny disturbance is not simply decaying at Reinj = 300: its energy grows by factors of 223 on the primary grid, 204 on the finer grid and about 1590 for seed 2 by 1200 s.

q0 changes the answer. At α = 0.5, Reinj = 300 and the same tiny η = 10⁻⁶, the mean-current error reaches 46.7%. The absolute error is 0.106 mm s⁻¹, compared with a 0.226 mm s⁻¹ change in the matched 2D run. At α = 1 the relative error is 0.245%. Small initial disturbances alone therefore do not specify the regime. The final 3D energy fraction is nonmonotone across this sweep: 3.71%, 0.158% and 0.822% for α = 0.5, 0.75 and 1, respectively.

Waves reduce disturbance amplification in the tested matched controls. For η = 10⁻⁶ and Reinj = 300, final GE is 223 with waves and 2463 without waves. At Reinj = 100 it is 0.0127 and 0.323, respectively: the disturbance decays in both. These comparisons retain the same initial Eulerian background and perturbations; they do not establish stability at all times or a general oceanic mixing law.

Why a single final fraction is not a closure criterion

History comparison · PDFHistory comparison · PNG

The proposed mean-vorticity description should therefore be assessed with the accumulated 3D correction and the measured mean-flow error, alongside instantaneous geometry. Turbulent transport and vortex-wave dynamics remain contributions to the same evolution. These diagnostics test when the 3D contribution is a small correction to the two-dimensional evolution.

What the moment budgets and numerical checks establish

The exact 2D and 2D3C runs have zero transverse-dependent q-production and agree in their mean currents to roundoff. This confirms the null test: nonzero v is compatible with the two-dimensional q equation. Conversion through −⟨(q−q0)w∂zq0 can remain nonzero in 2D and must be kept distinct from the 3D source T. Signed production, absolute production and native mean feedback are retained separately in the per-run budgets.

The defensible conclusion is a background-dependent, finite-time range in which weak 3D feedback permits an approximately autonomous 2D mean evolution. A broader map in (u*, us, u′) requires independent changes to wave and eddy speeds and, for u*, a specified surface-stress forcing. These simulations do not by themselves demonstrate a vertical tracer-transport barrier or place the transient Ellingsen packet in that range.
