Does added wind disrupt an established jet?

Two three-dimensional branches start from the same current jet that developed from a monotonic mean profile. The control continues the original dynamics. The other adds a surface wind stress and a spatially uniform return force that balances its net momentum input. Stokes drift, viscosity, the maintained background and the original boundary gradients remain identical.

Selected state: the bounded Re=300, initially tiny-3D case at 600 s has a resolved interior maximum near 0.388 m depth, about 1.20 mm/s prominence and 0.127 m width. It already contains a finite transverse disturbance; no new seed is imposed. The question is retention or erosion of this generated jet, not whether an arbitrary inherited mean maximum proves jet formation. “No additional wind” retains the parent’s prescribed background boundary gradients.

What can we conclude?

Both verified continuations are required before assessing the effect of added wind.

Compare the temporal ordering of roll growth, three-dimensional feedback and jet erosion with the control. Actual prominence and fixed-shape projection are shown beside direct laminar stress response; neither a profile change nor a large 3D energy fraction alone identifies a mechanism. Persistence of the jet under added wind would constrain this selected forcing and state, not rule out competition at other stresses.
